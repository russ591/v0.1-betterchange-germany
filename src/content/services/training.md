### How we actually run a training day

Most corporate training is built around a slide deck and a lecture. Ours isn't. Every Better Change course is designed and delivered using **Training from the Back of the Room (TBR)**, a brain-science-based approach to adult learning that several of our trainers are certified practitioners in.

The premise is simple: people don't learn by sitting still and listening to someone talk at a screen. They learn by doing, moving, talking to each other, and struggling productively with something before being told the answer. TBR is built around six research-backed principles that shape how every course runs:

- **Images over words.** Concepts get sketched, modelled, and shown, not just described in a bullet list.
- **Talking over listening.** Participants explain ideas back to each other constantly, not just to the trainer.
- **Movement over sitting.** Rooms get rearranged, people stand up, work happens on walls and floors, not just in chairs.
- **Writing over reading.** Participants capture their own understanding in their own words, rather than copying slides.
- **Shorter over longer.** Content comes in focused chunks with real breaks, not a continuous four-hour block.
- **Different over same.** Formats change throughout the day, simulation, discussion, small-group work, so attention doesn't flatline.

In practice, this means fewer slides and more simulations, games, and hands-on exercises that put people in a situation resembling their actual work, then let them work through it together. A Scrum course might run an entire mock sprint. A Kanban course might have participants physically build and break a flow system with sticky notes before ever opening a laptop. The goal isn't to entertain, it's to make the learning stick well enough that people can actually apply it back at work on Monday, not just recognise the vocabulary.
