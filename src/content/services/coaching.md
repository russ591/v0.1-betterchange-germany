### Coaching that builds capability, not dependency

A good coaching engagement should make itself unnecessary over time. Our coaches measure success partly by what a team or leader no longer needs them for, not by how indispensable they've made themselves. That means being deliberate about which of several coaching stances to use in a given moment, rather than defaulting to the same approach regardless of the situation:

- **Coaching** — asking the questions that help someone reach their own insight, rather than supplying the answer.
- **Teaching** — when a genuine skill or knowledge gap needs filling before anything else is possible.
- **Mentoring** — sharing relevant experience directly, when that's actually what's useful.
- **Advising** — offering a clear recommendation, when the situation genuinely calls for one.

Knowing which stance a moment calls for, and switching between them honestly, is a large part of what separates coaching that changes how a team or leader works from coaching that's really just consulting wearing a different hat.

### Team coaching

Self-organising, high-performing teams that are genuinely accountable for their own commitments don't happen by accident. Our coaches work directly with teams to build the trust, working agreements, and conflict-handling habits that make self-organisation actually function, not just get declared. This includes facilitated retrospectives that produce real change rather than a list nobody acts on, and working through the specific dysfunctions, whatever they happen to be, that are currently holding a team back.

### Leadership coaching

Leaders set the direction and the conditions for everyone around them, and knowing when to intervene, and when not to, is one of the hardest parts of the job. Our leadership coaching is one-to-one, built around the actual situation a leader is facing, not a generic curriculum. It ranges from ongoing personal sparring on specific decisions and dynamics, to more structured leadership development for people newly stepping into bigger roles.

### Scrum Master mentoring and embedded support

Sometimes a team needs a Scrum Master before it has the internal capability to grow its own. We can embed one of our coaches with a team for a defined period, doing the job directly while deliberately building the skills and habits that let someone internal take over when the engagement ends. This isn't meant to be permanent outsourcing, it's meant to leave a genuinely more capable team behind.

### Long-term engagements

Coaching that actually changes how people work takes longer than a single workshop. Most of our coaching engagements run over months, not days, with a deliberate arc: understand the real situation, work alongside the team or leader directly, and gradually step back as capability genuinely builds.
