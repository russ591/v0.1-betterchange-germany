  <h2>Key Points of the Interview</h2>

  <h3>1. The Scaling Wall: When Organic Communication Breaks Down</h3>
  <p>The biggest challenge Maja identified was the transition from a small, connected team to a larger organisation where informal communication channels stop working. Leaders in this position often respond by taking on more themselves, absorbing ambiguity, solving problems, shielding the team. It feels like leadership. But it is actually a bottleneck in disguise.</p>

  <h3>2. From Firefighting to Designing</h3>
  <p>The most meaningful shift Maja experienced was learning to pause before acting. Rather than rushing to solutions, she started prioritising a clear understanding of the current situation first, mapping where the team actually was before deciding where it needed to go. This reframing moved her from a reactive firefighter to a proactive designer of processes and environments.</p>

  <h3>3. What a Coach or Mentor Really Does</h3>
  <p>When asked what she would tell a leader on the fence about hiring a coach, Maja was clear: a coach is not someone who always gives you answers. They are a thought partner and a passionate designer of sustainable environments, helping you understand why things work or don't work, how the environment shapes your team's behaviour, and how to build collaboration and ownership into the way you work, rather than bolting them on as afterthoughts.</p>

  <h2>Why This Matters Today</h2>
  <p>Many leaders today are facing exactly what Maja described: organisations growing faster than the systems designed to support them, teams that need more than good intentions to collaborate effectively, and a creeping sense that working harder is not the same as working better.</p>
  <p>The shift from individual problem-solver to systemic designer is one of the most important (and most difficult) transitions a leader can make. It requires stepping back, asking better questions, and building the conditions for others to thrive. That is precisely the kind of work that a good coaching or mentoring relationship makes possible.</p>
  <p>If you recognise yourself in Maja's story, it might be time to have a conversation.</p>
