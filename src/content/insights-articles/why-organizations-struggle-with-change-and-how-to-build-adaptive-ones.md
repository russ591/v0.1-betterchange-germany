  <h2>Key Takeaways</h2>

  <h3>1. Organizations Are Complex Systems — Not Machines</h3>
  <p>Change initiatives often fail because organizations are treated as predictable machines. In reality, they are complex human systems — interconnected networks of people, beliefs, and relationships. What works in one context may not in another. Adaptability is a capability, not an end state.</p>

  <h3>2. Empirical Learning Over Upfront Planning</h3>
  <p>Rigid plans rarely survive contact with reality. Instead, organizations must embrace empiricism:</p>
  <ul>
  <li><strong>Transparency:</strong> Make goals, progress, and challenges visible.</li>
  <li><strong>Inspection:</strong> Regularly assess what's working and what's not.</li>
  <li><strong>Adaptation:</strong> Adjust based on evidence, not assumptions.</li>
  </ul>
  <p>This approach mirrors driving a car — you don't plan every turn in advance; you <strong>inspect and adapt as you go.</strong></p>

  <h3>3. Leadership as the Engine of Adaptation</h3>
  <p>Leadership isn't about approving plans; it's about creating the conditions for learning and experimentation. Leaders must:</p>
  <ul>
  <li><strong>Define clear, outcome-focused goals</strong> (not just outputs).</li>
  <li><strong>Remove impediments</strong> and foster psychological safety.</li>
  <li><strong>Engage actively</strong> in the improvement process, not just in status updates.</li>
  </ul>

  <h3>4. Focus on Outcomes, Not Outputs</h3>
  <p>Avoid goals like "10 Agile teams by year-end" if they don't tie to a clear business impact. Instead, ask: <strong>What problem are we solving?</strong> Use <strong>leading and lagging indicators</strong> to track progress and impact.</p>

  <h3>5. Treat Change as a Flow of Improvement Work</h3>
  <p>Use tools like <strong>improvement backlogs</strong> and <strong>Kanban boards</strong> to visualise and prioritise experiments. Start with small, safe-to-fail experiments to build momentum and reduce "change fatigue."</p>

  <h2>Why This Matters</h2>
  <p>Change is inevitable, but learning how to change is a competitive advantage. Organizations that treat change as a continuous learning process — not a one-off project — build resilience and adaptability.</p>
