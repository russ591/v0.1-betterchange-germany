  <p>Flight Levels is often described in abstract terms — a thinking model, a coordination framework, a way of connecting strategy with execution. In this webinar, hosted by Agile Boston, Konrad Pogorzala brings it down to earth.</p>

  <p>Drawing on his experience as a Fellow at Better Change, Konrad walks through how Flight Levels work in practice: what they look like when implemented, what the coordination meetings actually involve, and how organisations use them to break down the silos that agile team-level improvements consistently fail to address.</p>

  <h2>What the session covers</h2>

  <p>The webinar focuses on three practical areas. First, how Flight Levels create transparency across the organisation — not just within teams, but between teams and between the operational and strategic layers. The question "what are we actually working on, and is it the right thing?" sounds simple. Getting a reliable answer to it across a complex organisation is not.</p>

  <p>Second, how Flight Levels enable empowerment without chaos. One of the persistent tensions in agile transformations is between team autonomy and organisational coherence. Flight Levels offer a practical approach to maintaining both — teams make decisions about how they work, while the coordination layer ensures those decisions are connected to broader priorities.</p>

  <p>Third, how to identify and address systemic barriers to flow. Many of the obstacles to effective delivery are not within any single team's control. They are structural — dependencies, approval processes, misaligned priorities, resource contention. Flight Levels make these visible at the right level, where something can actually be done about them.</p>

  <h2>Who should watch</h2>

  <p>This session is most useful for leaders, managers, and agile coaches who are working in complex environments where team-level agile practices are already established but the expected organisational improvements have not materialised. If you have good teams but still struggle with predictability, alignment, or the connection between strategic intent and daily work, this is a relevant conversation.</p>
