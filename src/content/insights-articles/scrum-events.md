  <p>The five Scrum events are not a collection of required meetings. They are a system — designed to create inspect-and-adapt opportunities at the right frequency for each level of the work. Understanding them as a system rather than as individual ceremonies changes how you approach each one.</p>

  <h2>The Sprint</h2>

  <p>The Sprint is the container for all other events. Everything that happens in Scrum happens within a Sprint — planning, daily coordination, review, retrospective, and the actual delivery work itself. Sprints are fixed in length (one month or less, with most teams using one or two weeks) and run continuously: one ends, the next begins immediately.</p>

  <p>The fixed length is not arbitrary. It creates a rhythm that makes planning, progress, and improvement all more predictable. Teams that extend or shorten Sprints ad hoc undermine the consistency that makes the Sprint cadence valuable.</p>

  <h2>Sprint Planning</h2>

  <p>Sprint Planning answers three questions: why is this Sprint valuable, what can the team accomplish, and how will the work get done. The "why" question produces the Sprint Goal — the single objective that gives the Sprint its purpose and guides the team's decisions throughout. The "what" question produces the Sprint Backlog — the items the team selects from the Product Backlog. The "how" question produces a plan for delivering those items.</p>

  <p>Sprint Planning is often treated primarily as a selection exercise: which items go in, which stay out. The Sprint Goal tends to get less attention. This is the wrong emphasis. The Goal is what allows the team to make adaptive decisions during the Sprint. Without it, any change to the plan during the Sprint becomes a problem to be resolved by escalation rather than a decision the team can make themselves.</p>

  <h2>The Daily Scrum</h2>

  <p>The Daily Scrum is fifteen minutes for the Developers to inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt their plan for the next twenty-four hours. It is not a status report to the Scrum Master, and it is not a chance for the Product Owner to raise new requirements.</p>

  <p>The value of the Daily Scrum comes from its function as a daily adjustment mechanism — catching drift early rather than discovering it at the Sprint Review. Teams that use it well tend to raise blockers earlier, coordinate dependencies more effectively, and maintain a more accurate shared picture of where the Sprint stands.</p>

  <h2>The Sprint Review</h2>

  <p>The Sprint Review is a working session, not a presentation. The Scrum Team and stakeholders collaborate on what the team has built, what feedback it generates, and what that means for what happens next. The Product Backlog is typically updated based on this conversation.</p>

  <p>The Sprint Review is where the empirical process of Scrum becomes tangible: real work is inspected by real stakeholders, and the product's direction adapts based on what is learned. Teams that treat it as a sign-off ceremony rather than a genuine collaborative conversation lose most of its value.</p>

  <h2>The Sprint Retrospective</h2>

  <p>The Sprint Retrospective is the team's opportunity to examine how they work and identify specific improvements. It is the last event of the Sprint — after the Review, before the next Sprint begins — which positions it as the bridge between what was learned from the product this Sprint and what the team will do differently in the next.</p>

  <p>The Retrospective is the event most commonly abbreviated when Sprints get busy. This is the opposite of rational. The Retrospective is precisely the mechanism that improves all the other events — including the ones that were too long, too unfocused, or too frustrating this Sprint. Cutting it to save time tends to mean the problems that caused the time pressure recur next Sprint.</p>

  <h2>How the events connect</h2>

  <p>Each event depends on the previous one being done well. A weak Sprint Goal in Planning makes the Daily Scrum harder — the team lacks a clear reference point for their daily decisions. A weak Daily Scrum makes the Sprint Review less informative — problems that could have been identified and resolved mid-Sprint arrive at Review as delivery failures. A weak Sprint Review makes Retrospective less useful — there is less shared understanding of what actually happened to examine and improve.</p>

  <p>The events are not independent. They are a linked system, and the quality of each shapes the quality of the next.</p>
